Curator's Description

Painted in June 1889, The Starry Night stands as Vincent van Gogh's quintessential masterwork. Created from the east-facing window of his asylum room in Saint-Rémy-de-Provence shortly after his severe breakdown, the canvas depicts an idealized nocturnal landscape infused with intense subjective emotion rather than photographic realism.

The nocturnal sky dominates the upper two-thirds of the canvas, rendered in rhythmic, curving swirls of cobalt blue, ultramarine, and luminous golden yellows. Eleven incandescent stars and an exaggerated crescent moon radiate through concentric vortexes of light, creating an ecstatic celestial dance.

Anchoring the turbulent composition in the foreground is a towering, flame-like cypress tree, traditionally symbolic of mourning and eternity, rising to bridge the earthly village with the transcendent heavens. Van Gogh's expressive impasto brushstrokes and fearless use of vibrant color fundamentally paved the way for 20th-century Expressionism.
