Curator's Description

Often referred to affectionately as "The Mona Lisa of the North", Johannes Vermeer's Girl with a Pearl Earring is not a standard formal commissioned portrait, but a tronie—a seventeenth-century Dutch study of an anonymous face expressing a captivating character or exotic disguise.

The young girl is depicted against a deep, dark glaze background that pushes her radiant features forward into dramatic intimacy. She turns over her shoulder to gaze directly at the observer with large wide eyes and lips parted slightly as if about to speak. She wears an oriental turban combining expensive lapis lazuli ultramarine with mustard yellow drapery.

The focal point of the composition is the enormous droplet pearl earring dangling beside her neck. With astonishing economy of brushwork, Vermeer captured the lustrous pearl with merely two quick strokes of lead white paint: a bright crescent highlight at the top reflecting the studio window, and a softer gleam along the underside catching the glow of her white collar.
